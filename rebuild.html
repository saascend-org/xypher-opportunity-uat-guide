Rebuilding your pipeline

A tickable plan for re-entering your open Zoho deals by hand — in the right order, with realistic timings, and without inventing qualification history you never had.

You are re-entering open deals by hand rather than migrating them. Done in the right order this is straightforward, and you end up with better data than a migration would have given you — because every deal gets looked at by the person who owns it.

Why re-entering is genuinely fine

The stage gates do not fire when a record is created. You enter each deal at the stage it is actually at, and you are never asked to invent qualification history for stages the deal has already been through. You will be asked for that stage’s exit criteria the next time you move it — which is the right moment, because by then it should be real.

Proven, not assumed

This is the claim the whole approach rests on, so it was tested rather than reasoned about: deals were created at all four open stages with only the basics filled in, as an ordinary sales user, and all four saved. The two closed stages correctly refused. The test runs automatically, so it cannot quietly stop being true.

Order of work, and why

  1. Accounts first. A deal cannot exist without its customer. Check the Account is already there before creating one — many of your customers are already loaded. Search on part of the name, not the full legal name.

  2. Contacts second. You need at least the Economic Buyer, and past Qualify the Champion. Create them on the Account before you start the deal, or you will be interrupted halfway through.

  3. Opportunities third. One at a time, through the screen.

  4. Products last. Only strictly needed before you close a deal Won — but add them as you go if you know them, because it is the step everyone forgets.

Each deal, click by click

  1. Opportunities tab → New.

  2. Opportunity Name: Customer - Service - Year.

  3. Account: search part of the name. Do not create a duplicate.

  4. Close Date: the genuine expected decision date.

  5. Stage: where the deal really is today.This is the step that saves you the most work. Do not default to Qualify.

  6. Amount, Type, Business Unit, Product / Service Line.

  7. Next Step and a Next Step Date in the future.Non-negotiable. Without it the deal cannot move at all later.

  8. Save. It saves with qualification fields empty.

  9. Now add what you genuinely know: Economic Buyer, Pain, Success Metric, Funding Route and their grades.Grade honestly — see below.

Grade honestly

If the buyer has not told you the pain in their own words, do not mark that they have. The grades exist to show where a deal is thin. A pipeline of optimistically graded deals is worth less than no grades at all, and the first forecast review will find it.

Four traps specific to bulk re-entry

Trap 1 — won deals with a near contract end date

Do not enter a won deal with a contract end date in the next 90 days without expecting a renewal. The automation will generate one. That is usually what you want — but if you are back-filling historical won deals you will create a wave of renewals nobody asked for. Talk to us before entering closed-won history.

Trap 2 — framework deals need the framework name

If Procurement Route is Framework Call-off or Mini-Competition, Framework Name is mandatory on every save — not just on a stage change. Have the lot number to hand before you start those, or the record will not save at all. (Tested: it blocks on creation.)

Trap 3 — bid weightings

Do not set Price Weighting and Quality Weighting unless you know both. If both are set they must total 100, and a half-remembered tender weighting will just block your save.

Trap 4 — forecast category and closed-lost history

Leave the forecast category alone; it defaults from the stage, and overriding it needs a reason and a manager’s name. And do not re-enter Closed Lost history unless we have asked — it needs a loss reason on creation and cannot be reopened afterwards.

How long to allow

A deal you know well, contacts already there2–3 minutes

A framework deal, lot number to look up7–8 minutes
Twenty dealsAbout an hour

Do them in blocks rather than all at once. The qualification grades need thought, and quality degrades fast when this is done as a chore.
