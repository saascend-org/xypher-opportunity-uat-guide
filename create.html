Part one

Creating a deal

What Salesforce genuinely requires, why you should enter a deal at its real stage, and how contact roles look after themselves.

What you actually need to save a new deal

Very little. Salesforce itself only insists on four things:

FieldWhat to put
Opportunity NameA real convention — Customer - Service - Year. Consistency matters more than the exact format
Close DateYour genuine expected decision date. Not a comfortable one
StageWhere the deal really is today. See below — this is the important one
AccountThe customer. Create the Account first if it is not already there

Everything else can wait. The gates fire when you move stages, not when you save.

Create the deal at the stage it is genuinely at

This is the most useful thing on this site, and it is what makes rebuilding a pipeline by hand realistic rather than miserable.

Of the twelve rules governing opportunities, eight only fire when the stage changes. They do not fire when a record is created. A deal created directly at Proposal / Bid Submitted does not have to satisfy the Qualify or Solution Validation exit criteria first.

The point

You do not have to fake history. Put the deal in at its real stage, with what you actually know.

Two exceptions, because they check the stage itself rather than a change to it:

  • Closed Won needs Contract/PO Number, Signed Date, Contract Start, Contract End and Win Reason — even on creation.
  • Closed Lost needs Loss Reason and Loss Detail — even on creation.

The catch, so it does not surprise you

Creating at a late stage is free. Moving on from it is not. The exit gate for whatever stage you land on applies the moment you try to advance. A deal entered at Proposal will, when you next move it, need the full Proposal exit set — including all eight MEDDPICC elements. Enter it where it really is, and you will be asked for exactly the information you should already have.

Contact roles — you do not maintain these by hand

Set the Economic Buyer and Champion lookup fields and the contact roles are created for you. Change either lookup and the existing role is repointed rather than duplicated, so you end up with one role, not two.

Roles available if you add others by hand: Business User, Decision Maker, Economic Buyer, Champion, Economic Decision Maker, Evaluator, Executive Sponsor, Influencer, Technical Buyer, Other.

Worth testing

Set an Economic Buyer, save, and check the Contact Roles list. Then change it to a different contact and confirm you still have exactly one Economic Buyer role, now pointing at the new person.
